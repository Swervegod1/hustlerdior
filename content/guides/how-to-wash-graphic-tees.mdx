Wash graphic streetwear tees **inside out**, in **cold water**, on a gentle cycle (or hand wash), and **air dry**—heat and rough tumbling are what crack and dull most prints. Skip fabric softener and high-heat irons on the artwork; treat the graphic like the reason you bought the tee, not an afterthought.

## Why prints crack (plain English)

A cracked graphic is rarely “bad luck.” It’s usually heat, abrasion, or harsh chemistry wearing the print film or ink layer faster than the blank.

### Heat, abrasion, and harsh chemistry — not bad luck

High dryer heat, hot wash, and direct ironing cook and flex the print until it fractures. Abrasion from zippers, rivets, and drum friction scuffs the face of the art. Softeners and aggressive additives can leave residue or stress some finishes. Cold water, gentle motion, and low (or no) heat slow that aging down.

### Inside-out is non-negotiable for front and back hits

Flip the tee before it hits the machine. Inside-out washing keeps the artwork off the drum and away from other garments, so the graphic takes less scrape on every cycle. Front hits, back hits, and oversized chest prints all benefit the same way.

## The default wash routine

### Cold water + gentle / hand wash

Use cold water and a gentle cycle—or hand wash if the piece is a rotation favorite. Cold keeps the print and blank from shocking each other with heat. Gentle reduces the thrash that abrades ink and film.

### Mild detergent; skip softener on printed areas

Mild detergent is enough. Skip fabric softener on printed pieces unless a specific product care label says otherwise. Softeners aren’t a free upgrade for graphics; they can leave residue and stress some finishes.

### Separate zippers and hardware that scrape graphics

Wash graphic tees away from heavy denim hardware, metal zippers, and rough bags. One sharp zipper across a chest print does more damage in a single load than a careful month of wear.

## Drying and finishing

### Air dry preferred; low heat only if you must machine dry

Air dry is the safest default. Hang or lay flat until dry. If you must use a dryer, use the lowest heat and pull the tee while it’s still slightly damp—repeated high heat ages prints faster than almost anything else in the laundry room.

### Never iron directly on the print

Don’t put a hot iron on the artwork. If you need to press the blank, work from the reverse side or use steam carefully so the soleplate never sits on the graphic.

## Oversized / drop-shoulder / heavyweight notes

### More canvas ≠ tougher print

Oversized, drop-shoulder, and heavyweight blanks give you more fabric and a bigger print stage. They do **not** make the graphic heat-proof. Same cold / inside-out / low-heat rules apply. Protect the print the same way you would on a lighter blank.

### Fit and print scale still matter on-body

Care keeps the graphic sharp; fit and print placement decide how it reads when you wear it. For cut and scale, use the [fit guide](/fit-guide).

## FAQ

### How do I wash a graphic tee so the print doesn’t crack?

Turn it inside out, wash cold on gentle (or hand wash), use mild detergent, and air dry. High heat and rough tumbling are the most common reasons prints crack early.

### Can I put a printed streetwear tee in the dryer?

Air dry is safest. If you must use a dryer, use the lowest heat and pull the tee while still slightly damp—repeated high heat ages prints faster.

### Should I wash graphic tees inside out?

Yes. Inside-out washing reduces abrasion on the artwork from other garments and the drum.

### Does fabric softener ruin graphic prints?

Softeners and harsh additives can leave residue and stress some print finishes. Stick to mild detergent and skip softener on printed pieces unless a product care label says otherwise.

### Do oversized or heavyweight graphic tees need different care?

The same cold / inside-out / low-heat rules apply. Extra fabric is a bigger canvas, not a tougher print—protect the graphic the same way.
