Balance the two big shapes by giving the outfit one clear anchor: keep the boxy tee's hem near the hip or just past the waistband, let baggy pants fall straight over the shoe, and add one defined point such as a belt, a cap, or a chunky shoe. Pick a chunky sneaker or boot that can hold the hem, let the pants stack once without dragging, and tuck only a small front corner (or leave the tee out) so the waist still reads.

## The one rule: two big shapes need one anchor

Wear your own rules. This is the one that keeps a big tee and big pants from turning into a single block.

Volume on top and volume on the bottom works when the outfit has one point of control. That anchor can be a defined hem, a visible waist, a shoe with some weight, or a single structured layer.

**The mirror check:** can you see where your waist is, or where your shoes are? If yes, it works. If neither one reads, add an anchor.

## Proportions: where the tee ends and the pants begin

- **Hem length.** A cropped-boxy tee ends at the waistband, a hip-length tee sits at the hip, and a longer one runs past it. Each changes how long your legs read.
- **Match the hem to the pants.** End the tee where the pants' volume starts. Avoid a hem that cuts the leg in half mid-thigh.
- **Rise matters.** High-rise baggy pants give the tee a clean shelf to sit on. Low-rise pants need a shorter tee or more shoe so the outfit doesn't turn into a long, blocky column.
- **Shoulders.** A wide shoulder against wide legs is fine when the neckline and hem stay clean.

For sizing, check the [fit guide](/fit-guide).

## Shoes that carry baggy pants

- **Chunky sneakers and boots** hold the hem and balance the volume. Slim shoes can disappear under wide pants.
- **Hem and shoe.** Pick one: a full break, one clean stack, or a slight crop that shows the shoe. Don't let the hem drag under the heel.
- **Skate and low-profile sneakers** work when the pants are cropped or cuffed so the shoe stays visible.
- **Boots and mid-tops** pair well with straight-leg baggy bottoms. Keep one heavy element at the feet.
- **Socks** can be a small anchor, visible between hem and shoe.

## Tuck, half-tuck, or no tuck

- **No tuck.** Works when the tee ends at the hip and the pants sit high enough to show a waist line.
- **Front-corner (half) tuck.** A small tuck of the front hem brings the waist back on a longer tee. Keep it loose.
- **Full tuck.** Only really works on a lighter, shorter tee with a belt. On a boxy cut it usually bunches.
- **Long tee, low-rise baggy pants.** Leave it out or half-tuck. A full tuck adds bulk right where the outfit is widest.

## Layering over and around the tee

- **Open overshirt or light jacket.** Keep it near hip length so the tee hem still shows. One layer, not three.
- **Hoodie, worn open or tied at the waist.** It works as an anchor. Browse [hoodies and layers](/collections/hoodies-layers).
- **Long sleeve under a short-sleeve boxy tee.** Keep the under-layer hem at or above the tee hem.
- **Outerwear volume rule.** If the tee and pants are both big, pick a cleaner, shorter outer layer.

## Bottoms: pick your baggy

- **Baggy or wide-leg jeans** are the default. Vary the tone against the tee instead of matching it.
- **Cargo pants** add visual volume with their pockets. Keep the tee clean and the shoe simple.
- **Sweatpants and joggers** are the casual end. Use a defined cuff to avoid a shapeless column.
- **Wide-leg trousers** are the dressed-up end. A boxy tee against a sharper trouser makes a contrast look.
- **Baggy knee-length shorts** are the warm-weather version. The hem and shoe logic stays the same.

## Three looks to copy

**Look 1: The clean column.** Hip-length boxy tee, straight baggy jeans, chunky sneaker, no tuck. Why it works: the tee hem lands where the jeans' volume starts, and the shoe holds the bottom.

**Look 2: The shelf.** Shorter boxy tee, high-rise wide-leg cargos or trousers, boots, belt visible. Why it works: the high rise makes a clear waist line for the tee to sit on.

**Look 3: The layered throw.** Boxy tee, open overshirt or a hoodie tied at the waist, baggy sweats cuffed over a skate shoe. Why it works: the cuff keeps the shoe visible and the layer adds one defined shape.

## FAQ

### How do you style an oversized boxy tee with baggy pants?

Give the outfit one clear anchor so two big shapes don't blur together: a tee hem near the hip, a visible waist, or a chunky shoe. Let the pants fall straight over the shoe, and keep the rest of the outfit simple.

### What shoes go with an oversized tee and baggy pants?

Chunky sneakers and boots are the safest because they hold the hem and balance the volume. Low-profile skate shoes work when the pants are cropped or cuffed so the shoe stays visible. Avoid slim shoes under very wide pants, where they can disappear.

### How do you balance proportions with a boxy tee and wide-leg pants?

Make sure the tee hem ends where the pants' volume begins and that you can see either your waist or your shoes. A higher rise, a slightly shorter tee, or a defined belt line keeps the outfit from reading as one long block.

### Should you tuck an oversized tee into baggy pants?

Usually not fully. A hip-length boxy tee looks right untucked. If the tee is long, a small front-corner tuck brings back the waist line. A full tuck only works on a lighter, shorter tee with a belt, and tends to bunch on a boxy cut.

### How do you layer an oversized tee with baggy pants?

Keep to one clean layer over the tee, such as an open overshirt, light jacket, or a hoodie worn open or tied at the waist. Choose a layer that ends near the hip so the tee hem still shows, and skip stacking multiple bulky layers on top of baggy bottoms.

### What kind of baggy pants work best with a boxy tee?

Baggy or wide-leg jeans are the easy default. Cargos add visual volume, so keep the tee and shoe simple. Joggers and sweatpants work with a defined cuff. Wide-leg trousers give a sharper contrast to a boxy tee.

### Can you wear an oversized boxy tee with baggy shorts?

Yes. The same rules apply: keep the tee hem near the hip, choose a shoe with some weight, and avoid letting the tee and shorts end at the same point.
