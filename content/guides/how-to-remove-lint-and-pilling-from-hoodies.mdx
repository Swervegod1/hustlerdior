Lift **loose lint** with a lint roller or tape on **plain fabric only**. Take **pills** off the flat fleece with a fabric shaver or comb, using light, slow passes. Never run any tool over a print or embroidery, and don't overdo one spot or you'll thin the fabric. Test on an inside seam first. To slow it down, wash inside out, cold, and air dry.

## Lint vs. pilling: what you're actually fixing

Two different problems. Two different fixes.

### Lint is loose fuzz on the surface

Lint is loose fibers or fuzz, often picked up from other items in the wash, sitting on top of the fabric. It lifts off easily.

### Pilling is friction

Pills are short fibers tangled into small balls by rubbing. Underarms, cuffs, the pouch, and anywhere the fabric rubs. It's common on fleece and cotton-blend knits, and it isn't a defect by itself. A pilled hoodie isn't a bad hoodie.

### Why fleece sheds early

Brushed or fleecy fabric can release loose fibers in the early washes and then settle down. We won't put a timeline on it. It varies. We also don't state fiber content or fabric construction for individual pieces here. Check the product page.

## Before you start (the 60-second safety check)

A minute now saves the hoodie.

### Lay it flat and mark the no-go zones

Put the hoodie flat on a clean, hard surface. Look at it. Find every print and any embroidery. Those are your no-go zones.

### Test on an inside seam

Try your tool on an inside seam or hem first. If it snags or roughs up the fabric there, it will do worse on the front.

### Check the care label

Read the care label on your piece, and see the product page for anything specific to that item. Ordering and made-to-order details are on [Help](/help).

## Remove surface lint

Easy first. Start here.

### Roller or tape, plain fabric only

Press a lint roller or a piece of tape onto plain fabric and lift. That's it.

### Keep adhesive off the art

Adhesive can tug at the edges of a print and at embroidery threads. Keep rollers and tape off the graphic.

### Optional: a clean, slightly damp hand

For stray lint, a clean, slightly damp hand wiped in one direction can lift it. Simple, general, no tools.

## Remove pills without thinning the fabric

Light touch. Stop early.

### Fabric shaver or de-piller

Work on flat, tensioned fabric. Short, slow passes. Keep the guard on. Stop the moment the pills are gone, and **don't repeat over the same spot.**

### Fabric comb or sweater stone

Gentler, good for small areas. Light strokes, one direction.

### Scissors for the odd stubborn pill

Snip. Don't pull. Pulling yanks fiber and can leave a snag or a hole.

### Why overdoing it hurts

De-pilling removes surface fiber. Do it too long in one place and the fabric thins out or looks rough. "Enough" is when the surface looks clean. Not when it looks brand new.

## Work around prints and embroidery (no-go zone rules)

The graphic is why you bought it. Protect it.

- **Never** run a shaver, comb, roller, or tape over a print or embroidery.
- Tension the fabric around the art. Work toward it from plain fabric and stop at the edge.
- If lint sits inside the art area, lift it by hand with clean fingertips or a soft, dry brush. Don't scrub.
- Embroidery stitches can snag. Keep tools and sharp edges clear.

We're not naming which pieces carry print versus embroidery, or how each is made. Look at the piece and the product page. If you can see it, treat it as a no-go zone.

## Keep it from coming back

A one-line recap, not the full routine. For print care, see [how to wash graphic tees](/guides/how-to-wash-graphic-tees).

### Wash habits

Wash inside out, cold, gentle, with mild detergent. Don't overload the drum.

### Separate the shedders and the scrapers

Keep hoodies away from lint-shedding items like towels and fresh cotton, and from rough hardware like zippers and rivets.

### Dry smart

Clean the dryer lint trap. Air dry when you can, and avoid high heat.

### Know the rub points

Bags, seat belts, and underarms are where pills start. Rotate your pieces so one hoodie isn't taking all the friction.

## When to stop (and when it's just the fabric)

### Stop if the surface looks thin or shiny

If the fleece looks thin or shiny after de-pilling, you've gone too far. Stop.

### Pilling that returns can be normal

Some knits pill again quickly. That's normal for them. If the fabric truly fails, contact support through [Help](/help). We're not making warranty promises here. Policies live on Help.

## FAQ

### How do I remove lint and pills from a hoodie?

Lift loose lint with a lint roller on plain fabric, then remove pills with a fabric shaver or comb using light passes on flat fleece. Test on an inside seam first and stop as soon as the surface looks clean.

### Will a fabric shaver damage my hoodie?

Used lightly, it removes surface pills. Used too long over one spot, it can wear the fabric thin or leave a rough patch, so keep passes short, keep the guard on, and don't repeat over the same area.

### Can I use a lint roller or de-piller on a printed or embroidered hoodie?

Only on plain fabric. Keep rollers, tape, shavers, and combs off the print and embroidery, since they can snag or lift the art. Work up to the edge from plain fabric and lift stray lint inside the art area by hand.

### Why does my hoodie pill?

Friction tangles short fibers into small balls, most often at underarms, cuffs, and wherever the fabric rubs. It's common on fleece and cotton-blend knits, and it doesn't automatically mean a defect.

### How do I stop my hoodie from getting lint and pills again?

Wash it inside out on a cold, gentle cycle, keep it apart from lint-shedding items like towels and from hardware that scrapes, don't overload the washer, and air dry when you can.
