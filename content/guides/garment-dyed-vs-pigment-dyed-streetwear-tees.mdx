**Garment-dyed** is about *when* a tee gets its color: after the shirt is cut and sewn, as a finished garment. **Pigment-dyed** is about *what* colors it: pigment that sits on the surface of the fibers and fades gradually. The two overlap, so one tee can be both. Either way, expect soft color that changes with wear. Wash cold, inside out, and apart from light colors.

## Garment-dyed means the dye comes after the sewing

Stage, not chemistry. That's the whole idea.

### Dyed after cut-and-sew vs. fabric dyed before the shirt is made

A common route: the fabric is colored first, then the shirt is cut and sewn from it. Garment-dyed flips the order: the shirt gets built first, then the finished piece goes into the color.

### Why the whole garment takes the color (and why it reads soft and uneven)

When the finished tee is dyed, the seams, hems, and collar take color along with the body. That's why garment-dyed pieces tend to look a little softer and a little uneven. It's less "stiff and uniform," more "already lived in." That's a feature of the process, not a flaw in the tee.

## Pigment-dyed means color that sits on the fiber

Different question. Not *when*, but *how*.

### Pigment vs. other dyes, in one paragraph

Other dye types are absorbed into the fiber. Pigment works differently: it sits on the surface of the fiber. That surface layer is why pigment color wears down gradually and gives you that washed, vintage look.

### Why pigment-dyed color fades slowly into a vintage look

Surface color moves. Wear, friction, and washing take a little of it off over time, so the tee keeps softening instead of holding one fixed shade. Some people buy for exactly that. Others want a crisp, uniform black and should know what they're signing up for.

### The overlap: stage vs. dye type

Here's the part most explainers blur. Garment-dyed is the stage. Pigment is one kind of dye. A tee can be garment-dyed *with* pigment, garment-dyed with something else, or neither. They aren't opposites.

## Will it fade? Will it bleed or transfer?

Short answers: probably softens, and sometimes releases a little color.

### Fade is part of the look

Expect the color to soften with wear and washing. Don't think of it as a fixed shade you're trying to preserve. Think of it as a tee that keeps changing, slowly. Cold water, inside out, and air drying slow that down. Nothing stops it.

### Transfer: wash dark and saturated colors apart

Darker or heavily dyed pieces can rub or release color onto light clothing, bags, or furniture. It's most noticeable when the piece is new or wet. Wash dark and saturated colors separately from whites and pastels, especially the first few times. Don't toss a new black tee in with your light stuff.

### Color varies, piece to piece and screen to hand

Dyed garments don't come out identical. Color can read a little differently from one piece to the next, and screens never match what you hold. Treat what you see online as a guide, not a promise of an exact match.

## How to wash garment-dyed tees

Same simple routine. No tricks.

### The routine

- Turn it **inside out**.
- Wash **cold** on a **gentle** cycle.
- Use **mild detergent**.
- Keep it **away from light colors**, especially in the first washes.

### Dry it right

Air dry when you can. Hot dryer cycles speed up fading and can shrink the piece, so skip high heat.

## FAQ

### What does garment-dyed mean?

Garment-dyed means the tee is dyed after it has been cut and sewn, rather than being made from fabric that was dyed beforehand. The whole garment, seams included, takes the color, which usually gives a softer, slightly uneven, lived-in look.

### What is the difference between garment-dyed and pigment-dyed?

Garment-dyed describes the stage: dyeing the finished garment. Pigment-dyed describes the type of dye, where pigment sits on the surface of the fibers and fades gradually over time. The two can overlap, so a garment-dyed tee may or may not use pigment.

### Will a garment-dyed tee fade?

Expect the color to soften with wear and washing. That gradual softening is a big part of the look. Cold washing, inside out, and air drying slow it down.

### Can garment-dyed or pigment-dyed shirts bleed onto other clothes?

They can release some color, especially when new or when saturated. Wash dark and strongly colored pieces separately from whites and pastels, particularly in the first few washes.

### How do I wash a garment-dyed t-shirt?

Turn it inside out, wash cold on a gentle cycle with mild detergent, keep it away from light colors, and air dry. If the tee has a graphic, follow the graphic care steps as well, and always check the care label on your piece.
