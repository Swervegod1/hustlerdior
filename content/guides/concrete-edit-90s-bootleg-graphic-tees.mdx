## What “90s bootleg” means in streetwear culture (and what it doesn’t)

### Print attitude, bootleg-era energy, underground graphics

In buyer language, “bootleg 90s” usually points to bold type, unauthorized-era attitude, and high-impact graphics—the illicit print heat of that culture moment. It’s a *mood and memory*, not a manufacturing standard.

### Not counterfeits, not stolen logos, not warehouse theater

At Hustler Dior, bootleg energy means **original** Concrete Edit prints with that attitude. It does **not** mean selling counterfeits, copying other brands’ marks, inventing fake collabs, or running scarcity theater. If a design isn’t ours, it doesn’t ship as ours.

## The Concrete Edit graphic language

### Heavy typography

Type leads. Heavy letterforms, stacked statements, editorial weight. Graphics you can describe in one sentence.

### Dark tones / high-contrast prints

Dark grounds. High-contrast ink. Readable from across the room—not soft pastel logo mush.

### Unfiltered expression

Concrete Edit rules: unfiltered expression under independent DTC. Ambition you can wear. Not logo theater for its own sake.
