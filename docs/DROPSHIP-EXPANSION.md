## Pricing that exposes the real tradeoff

Trendsi displays separate dropship and bulk-wholesale prices. The calculations use the **dropship** price. Most overseas product pages opened with Germany selected, so displayed free shipping is not a verified U.S. quote. The Zenana page instead showed United States, $5.99 shipping, and a 2–5 business-day shipping indication; these still require a fresh account quote. [Zenana product page](https://www.trendsi.com/products/detail?id=395508&name=Zenana+Acid+Wash+Raw+Edge+French+Terry+Sweatshirt+with+Front+Pocket&skuId=2630272).

Two explicitly hypothetical scenarios accompany every new draft:

| Input | Base planning case | Higher-cost case |
|---|---:|---:|
| Item cost | Public displayed dropship price | Same observation |
| Shipping, overseas/unknown | $8.99 | $14.99 |
| Duty allowance, overseas/unknown | $2.00 | $5.00 |
| Shipping, Zenana U.S. listing | $5.99 | $9.99 |
| Duty allowance, Zenana U.S. listing | $0.00 | $0.00 |
| Payment fees | 3% + $0.30 assumed | Same |
| Reserve for expected return losses | 8% of merchandise revenue | Same |
| Target contribution | 35%, before advertising/overhead | Same |

Shipping and duty allowances are scenario inputs, not rates, tax advice, or verified landed costs. The return reserve is a percentage of revenue reserved for losses; it is not an assumed return rate. Sales tax is excluded from merchandise revenue. Real carrier surcharges, tariffs, unrecovered refunds, discounts, FX, packaging, and platform charges must replace or extend these assumptions.

```text
price floor = (item + shipping + duty allowance + fixed payment fee)
              / (1 - payment fee rate - return-loss reserve - target contribution)

contribution before ads = price × (1 - payment fee rate - return-loss reserve)
                         - item - shipping - duty allowance - fixed fee

max acquisition cost to retain 20% contribution
  = max(0, contribution before ads - 20% × price)
```

For the Zenana example, a $19.82 item plus $5.99 shipping produces a $48.36 minimum under these assumptions, rounded upward to a $48.99 test price. The supplier's displayed MSRP was $39.64. That mismatch is a reason to test customer willingness to pay, negotiate costs, or reject the item—not a reason to promise a 35% margin at MSRP. Bundle-level economics must be recalculated at order level; do not simply sum per-item acquisition budgets.

No selling prices were changed. `retailPriceCents` remains `null`. The review separates supplier MSRP, observed cost, calculated test prices, and any eventual approved retail price.
